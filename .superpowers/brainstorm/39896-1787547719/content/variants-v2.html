Why a "card per variant" instead of a table row

  • Variant Name is one free-text field — "256GB · Blue", "M · Red", "Large Pack", whatever fits the product. No forced Size+Color grid that breaks for non-apparel items.
  • Purchase Rate and Selling Price live on every variant — a 256GB phone and a 512GB phone are bought and sold at different prices; this is no longer possible to get wrong.
  • Expiry is per-variant too — if this SAME product has batch tracking needs that differ per variant (e.g. two colors received in different shipments with different expiry), it's captured right where the variant is, not in a separate disconnected Batches section.
  • A card layout (vs. a 5-column table row) has room to breathe — labels sit directly above their field, so nothing needs a legend/header row to be readable.
  • Barcode + Scan + Generate stays exactly where you'd expect it — top-left of each variant's field grid.

Trade-off

  • Takes more vertical space per variant than a table row — fine for 2-5 variants (the common case), could get long for a product with 20 variants; a "collapse to summary" affordance (click a variant card to fold it to one line showing just name+stock) can be added later if that becomes a real complaint.

What happened to the separate "Batches" toggle?

Folded into the variant card above (each variant has its own optional Expiry field) — because in practice, if a product has variants, expiry is almost always tracked per-variant, not per-product. For a product with NO variants but that DOES expire (e.g. a single-SKU medicine), Batches stays as its own simple toggle below, unchanged from before.
